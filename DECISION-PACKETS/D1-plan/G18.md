### (a) Witness-byte selections inside recorded schemas — permitted under the grant

1. The concrete generation identities `N` and `N+1` used in each record, given that the fate
   vocabulary ("generation N or generation N+1, never a mixture") is already recorded at

2. Which of the two recorded recovery outcomes each crash site takes — "resume/restore of an exact

3. The concrete content of the six EV-3 closure halves (`dependency`, `permission/confinement`,

4. Which of the seven EV-5 reachability roots each removal record exercises, and the order in which

5. The concrete lease/refcount values in EV-4 records, and whether reap is "explicit" or

6. The four platform tokens, quoted from `$.platforms` rather than restated.
7. Binding each authored payload to one of the 40 `g18-input-corpus.v2` initial-state ids
   (`inputCorpusId`, per the g21.v1 member shape).

   as a named open decision, do not choose.**
6. **A solver algorithm or a filesystem layout.** Violates `$.mechanismReservation.reserved[3..4]`
   and occupancy `$.failsIf[20]`. — **record as a named open decision, do not choose.**
7. **A D9 code, exit number, HostTermination, loss code, or purge result.** Violates occupancy
   `$.doesNot[9]` ("Does not design purge."), `$.doesNot[10]`, `$.failsIf[34]`, `$.failsIf[40]`; and
   `$.retainedEvidence[4].passProperty` says "Removal deactivates; it does not emit a typed PURGED
   sealed-Run result." — **record as a named open decision, do not choose.**
8. **A same-project concurrent-analyze class, a live-lease activation class, or a two-cache-writer
   class.** Violates `$.namedCorpusClasses[1].exactByteIntent` and `$.failsIf[39]`. — **record as a

9. **A DR-111 window number.** Violates `$.retainedEvidence[0].exactByteIntent` ("No-return is

10. **A Windows cell.** Violates `$.windowsStanding` and `$.failsIf[42]`. — **record as a named open

11. **Dropping `every` or `no-return` from the first corpus class.** Violates

### Risk

The reviewer-attack pattern maps onto G18 with one dominant failure mode. (a) *Inventing a vocabulary
the record does not carry* — this is nearly unavoidable for G18, because a crash-injection fixture is
naturally written as bytes on disk and every on-disk vocabulary G18 could use (journal record format,
lock file, lease file, quarantine directory, filesystem layout) is on the reserved list. The g23.v1
finding M3 ("hostRequestedDomainBaseline invents a Coverage-domain encoding vocabulary") is the exact
shape a reviewer would land on a G18 corpus that shipped a journal record layout, and the g23.v1
finding M2 (undeclared digest construction) applies again to the eight "digest-pinned" phrases in
the occupancy that name no construction. (b) *Collapsing two classes the occupancy keeps separate* —
`$.liveCellMemberSplit.notCollapsed` pre-names five collapses that fail, including "leases/refcounts
is not collapsed into process death", which is exactly the collapse a compact corpus would reach for
since `process-death` supplies EV-4; the g23.v2 finding M1 is the same defect. (c) *Classifying a
witness under a closed identifier it does not belong to* — the g21.v3/v4 finding `G21FXV3-M1` maps
onto the three-class boundary sentences: a process-death record filed as a G21 process-tree case, or
a repair-media crash filed as a G18 crash injection, is expressly excluded by
`$.namedCorpusClasses[2].exactByteIntent` and `$.namedCorpusClasses[0].exactByteIntent`. (d)
*Over-claiming closure* — the g23.v1 finding S4 maps onto
`leftoverDesignClosedIfAcceptedAndRecorded`: `OBL-G18-FX-AUTHORING` is measured true on two joins and
`OBL-ENCODING-RESERVED` stays true on DR-107 under the adopted C6, so a corpus that claims either
closed is over-claiming on its face. The G18-specific risk beyond those four is the DR-107 token: the
ROW twin's live token is `PROPOSED-CLOSED-FOR-REVIEW`, the GATE join's is `OPEN`, and both joins
carry an express "does not flatten" clause — a successor pair that pins one token to both rows fails
on the joins' own words before any fixture byte is examined.

## 7. Open questions

**OQ-G18-1:** Can a digest-pinned crash-injection record be authored without an on-disk journal
format? The three texts that make this ambiguous, side by side:

- `harness.DR-G18.lifecycle-generation-recovery.v4.json` `$.namedCorpusClasses[0].exactByteIntent`:
  "Once authored: per D-002 platform, digest-pinned crash injections at every named journal write,
  fsync, rename, and pointer transition, and at every migration prepare, commit, abort, and no-return
  boundary. Each record retains the injected site, the pre-crash generation identity, the
  post-recovery observer-visible generation (old or new, never mixed), and whether recovery resumed or
  restored an exact verified generation or fail-closed/quarantined."
- The same field, continuing: "This class names crash-injection sites. It does not choose an on-disk
  journal format, lock-file grammar, lease API, solver, filesystem layout, or reviewed-equivalent of
  atomic rename. Those mechanisms remain reserved."
- `g18-leftover-join.v6.json` `$.obligations[3].reason`: "This join does not invent those fixture
  bytes and does not invent a journal."

The record sequences the mechanism successor (occupancy `$.proposedLaterWork[4]`) and the fixture
cycle (`$.proposedLaterWork[3]`) without ordering them, while the ROW twin's
`$.proposedLaterWork[1]` conditions fixture authoring on types being "already closed" and the journal
type is not closed. Not resolved here.

**OQ-G18-2:** Can a `conflicting project locks` record be authored while lock production is
DR-111-blocked? `component-manifest-schemas.v11.json` `$.lockSchema.purpose` says "NO lock is
producible until DR-111 closes; this field describes the deferred record, not a presently producible
artifact." Occupancy `$.namedCorpusClasses[1].exactByteIntent` says the record "retains both lock
pin-sets (signed index snapshot, component/artifact closure, platform alternative, subprotocol/state
formats, and policy constraints as file 04 names them)" while also saying "It does not fork DR-103
lockSchema and does not produce a lock." Live file 04 line 67–68 names that pin-set. Whether a pin-set
record is or is not a lock for this purpose is not stated.

**OQ-G18-3:** Is the quarantine outcome authorable at all? `$.retainedEvidence[1].exactByteIntent`
makes quarantine a required half of EV-2 ("Quarantine is required") and reserves its format in the
same sentence ("on-disk quarantine format is reserved"). Occupancy `$.doesNot` and `$.failsIf` supply
no lawful placeholder standing for a reserved-format artifact, unlike G15's `blocked-on-ride` fate
vocabulary. This is the sentence the §4 standing rests on; whether a corpus may record the quarantine
*fate* without the quarantine *bytes* is not stated.

**OQ-G18-4:** Should the first G18 corpus be per-platform-copy (g23.v4 shape) or flat (g21.v1 shape)?
All three `namedCorpusClasses[*].exactByteIntent` strings open "Once authored: per D-002 platform",
and `g18-input-corpus.v2.json` already carries the platform token inside each of its 40 initial-state
ids — which reads as forty distinct payloads rather than ten payloads copied four ways. The record
does not say which.

**OQ-G18-5:** EV-5 names seven reachability roots in its `exactByteIntent` while file 04 lines
131–135 and EV-5's own `measures` field group them into five bullets. `$.failsIf[1]` fails a corpus
that omits "active/provisional generations or binding retention policy/tombstones" from the root set,

(7 × 4 platforms = 28) or one record covering all roots per platform (4) is not stated.
