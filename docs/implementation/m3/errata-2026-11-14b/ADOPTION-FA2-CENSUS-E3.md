**Substance, in plain words:**
- **The population-rule carrier (Part A, statements A1-A10).** A closure's binding is one member of the enumerator closure's file tree at `opensip-interface/symbol-census/population-rule.json`. Its bytes are exactly `{"populationRule":"<rule id>","schemaVersion":1}`: UTF-8, no whitespace, no final newline. It names exactly one rule id and is part of the closure's identity. Only the packager writes it, from one static source file. A closed table (A3) lists the rules a closure may be bound to, by sha256 of those exact bytes and by role subprotocol: `native.symbol-census-population.default.v1` (`6f2726992d65dfcf33700abcad964a9b1aad82178976ee2a2b508e66b7a84671`, `rust-semantic` only) and `native.symbol-census-population.typescript.v3` (`c10552a44dc8086d300fc647f8607eda22f7327f00560302d42469ca8b8f85c7`, `typescript-semantic` only). Any other bytes name an unknown rule, including the withdrawn v1 and v2. The carrier exists for every enumerator closure from adoption, and E1's interim "no binding means the default rule" reading ends then. A rule that only an adopted erratum states binds nothing until the FA-2 contract successor that states it is a `contractSuccessors` entry of the design lock (A9). A TypeScript provider's census may be admitted as `complete` only under the three conditions of A10.
- **Prospective Plan-time checks (A6).** From adoption, before any worker is spawned, the host checks for every enumerator closure that owes a census: (1) the member is present; (2) its sha256 is a row of A3; (3) that row binds the closure's role subprotocol; (4) the successor that states the row's rule is a `contractSuccessors` entry of the host's own design lock. Each failure takes the existing host-invariant refusal of section 9.8. No code, key or deficiency is registered.
- **The total replay classification (A7).** At replay, each retained enumerator closure gets exactly one binding class by one ordered procedure (conflict; A2 member; demonstrated historical binding; presented but not demonstrated; none). A member at the A2 path whose bytes are not the A2 form of a known rule, including malformed bytes, is *other id* and fails closed. A table of rows RT-1 to RT-7 by provider, class and census with its inventory, with seven outcomes O1 to O7 and three cited "cannot occur" reasons, gives every state exactly one row. A demonstrated historical binding keeps its rule through its retained evidence. An unrecognized representation fails closed with no producer or host-fault finding. Host-derived inventories carry no rule. The erratum's totality control checks 2,016 concrete states, each with exactly one row or one reason, and both negative controls fail as required.
- **Rule `native.symbol-census-population.typescript.v3` (Part C).** It is v2 (E2, by sha256 `56723546361dd29bed388b22837f57f2fbb4ae131e61a7a957544ab0ad6f031e`) unchanged, plus statement 4 and two sentences on the design note's F3. v2 is withdrawn before any use, within the tracked-source scope the erratum states (product main `e3cd0bf5` and architecture `06f966a1e`); it does not prove that no untracked artifact names v2. v2 stays in E2 as bound and is never bound to a closure.
- **What v3 adds over v2 (statement 4, Part B).** An export entry's declarations are classified one by one by syntax, whatever their number or order; the compiler's merged symbol decides nothing. The entry is a row when at least one declaration is not an alias. Each alias declaration exposes its own targets, found by a finite walk (cycles detected per path, a completed result reused on reconvergence, an intermediate alias taken as the one compiler symbol its name resolves to). A row is `exported` when it is an entry row, an alias target, or a bound subject with an export symbol. It is `unknown` only when a `default` or `export=` declaration has an expression value, or an alias declaration is unresolved. Outside statement 4, no `exported` value is law; F3 stays a design note. The 1,327 read-only probes at product `e3cd0bf5` give 513 cases that match and 814 fix-owed cases.
