## 2. Lead decisions

- **LD-D3A-1. macOS first, as D1a.** D1a implements only this host's macOS family, and refuses elsewhere with `PlatformUnsupported` (process.rs:9-11). D3a is written over D1a's API, with D3a-0's mechanisms in D1a's own module. Its Linux halves (`/proc/[pid]/statm` sampling, LX-9; the subreaper, LX-8) land with D1a-L on a Linux lane. Nothing in D3a claims Linux.
- **LD-D3A-2. The split** (§1, r2).
- **LD-D3A-3. The participant seam (r2, RF-1 and NBO-2).** Item 10 makes the provider-side participant "the only reader of fd1 and the only writer of fd0" (:379, citing CPC:253-254), and item 11 rule 3 says "the control plane holds no provider octets" (:405). So the loop never reads fd1 or writes fd0 itself:
  - **The trait.** `supervisor::Participant` is called synchronously by the loop:
    - `readable(&mut dyn Read)` when fd1 is readable: the participant makes **one** non-blocking `read` into its own buffer and feeds the bytes to its `FrameAccumulator` (D2b-1), whose prefix, digest and payload state survives `WouldBlock` and short reads; each complete frame goes to D2b-1's strict decode and then to the codec's admission and table;
    - `writable(&mut dyn Write)` when fd0 is writable and the participant has a pending write: **one** non-blocking `write` from its pending buffer at its offset, the offset advanced by the bytes written, `WouldBlock` leaving both unchanged;
    - `process(ProcessEvent)` for item 13's process events;
    - `close_input()` at the normal end (LD-D3A-15).
  - **The yield contract.** Each call makes at most one non-blocking read or write and returns. The loop then services the other descriptors, the exit source and the timers before the next call, so health, stderr, the exit and every deadline run between any two provider reads.
  - **What a call returns** is a `ParticipantStep`: running, wants-write, a terminal reached (with its kind), or FAULT; the admitted transitions that are item 15's progress (snapshot chunk acknowledged, `FactBatch`, `Coverage`/`CoverageV3`, stage change, :513); and **typed accounting**: the payload bytes of each admitted frame (the TS2 aggregate's input, item 16), and at fd1 EOF whether the accumulator is `at_boundary()` (a boundary EOF, which only the table may accept) or inside a frame (truncation, a protocol fault). It carries no payload bytes.
  - **The terminal output capability.** On a verified terminal, with every commitment recomputed (RPP:589-596; DLV:1138), the participant mints one `TerminalOutput`: opaque, not `Clone`, with no public constructor outside the participant's module, holding the spool and the terminal's view. The supervisor takes it once into the settlement, and item 20's gate either releases it to the admission owner (a clean settlement) or drops it (discard). A candidate count is never evidence: without a `TerminalOutput`, nothing is releasable.
  - **`ProcessEvent`** is D2b's type (LD-D2B-6). D3a needs it first, so **D3a-1 lands it in `provider_protocol.rs` with exactly LD-D2B-6's shape**, `ProcessEvent {ZeroExit, Eof, Fault(kind)}` with `kind` one of deadline, nonzero-exit, signal-death or stdout-byte, and with its mapping onto D2b-2's `table::Event` frames (`zero-exit`, `eof`, and the four `*PROCESS_FAULT` members, P3T:75-85, T2O:61-68). D2b-3 and D2b-4 consume it unchanged (Q2).
  - **The fixture participant.** D3a's tests drive a participant built from D2b-1's `FrameAccumulator` and D2b-2's table engine over a fixture child. It runs P3T's or T2O's rows on frame types alone and admits no payload. It is a test double, `#[cfg(test)]`, and never a codec.
- **LD-D3A-4. The control channel's acquisition.** D2a classifies complete frames only. D3a's reader reads fd4 non-blocking:
  - the 4-byte prefix first, and the length judged against the session's frame bound (`ControlSession::policy().frame_bound()`, 65,536) before any body byte is read;
  - then exactly the body into one reused buffer of at most bound + 4 bytes (D2a NBO-2's "bound-plus-four" buffer);
  - then `ControlSession::receive(ComponentToHost, frame)`.

  A frame split at every byte offset, and several frames in one read, reassemble the same (item 11's D5-C1 class, :415). An over-bound prefix goes to `receive` as the prefix alone, which refuses RF2 unbuffered, exactly as D2a-T3 classifies it. The host's frames go out through `ControlSession::send` only, which encodes, checks the policy and advances the session (D2a r2).
  - **The control observations D3a advances** (`observations_mut`): `hello_seen` after the host's hello is sent; `confirmed_tuples` from the accepted `helloAck`; `selected_tuple` after `select`; `pending_health_report` for item 15's nonce. Each is what D2a's session leaves to "the supervisor to advance" (control_protocol.rs:1000-1003).
- **LD-D3A-5. Settlement: the single-owner equivalent of item 12's compare-and-swap (r2, NBO-1).** Item 12 says the one transition into `Settled` "is guarded by a compare-and-swap" (:435). The lead accepts this single-owner equivalent, and states its invariant:
  - **One owner.** The loop thread holds the only `Supervision`. A cross-thread request (J's signal handler, D3b's revocation) reaches it only as an appended event through one bounded channel, never by touching the state.
  - **A terminal guard.** Every transition checks the state first; `Settled` accepts no transition, and an event after it is counted and dropped.
  - **One construction.** `Settlement` has a private constructor, called only inside the one function that performs the transition into `Settled`, which sets the guard in the same step. It is not `Clone`, and the supervision hands it out by value once (`Option::take`), so a second settlement can be neither constructed nor taken.
  - **Controls:** a re-entrant transition request from inside a join, a late event after settlement, and `Drop` after the settlement was taken, each leave exactly one settlement and no second effect.
- **LD-D3A-6. D3a's fault ladder.** Item 12 requires every path to reach `Settled` (:435), and items 18 and 19 are D3b's. So D3a runs one ladder for every D3a **fault** cause, which is the fault ladder's skeleton (J-5, item 19, :637-641). (r2, RF-3) The normal end is not a fault and never takes this ladder from its start; its grace expiry enters it at T-2 (LD-D3A-15):
  1. **T-1:** control `cancel {reason: deadline}` for the deadline, `cancel {reason: supervisor-fault}` for every other cause, if fd3 is writable (:638);
  2. **T-2:** `SIGTERM` to the group (`Child::signal_group`), with no grace (:639);
  3. **T-3:** `SIGKILL` to the group after the 1 s escalation (item 14, :493), then the J-3 drain (:465, :495);
