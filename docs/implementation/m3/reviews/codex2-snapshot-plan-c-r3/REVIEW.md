## Required findings

### C3-R1 — Join carrier presence as well as its digest

**Location:** item 1, lines 103–110, 117 and 129–134; item 16, step 9, line 726.

**Problem.** The digest-only handoff is sufficient for two present captures, but the rule only compares the bytes of an `opensip.json` that the walk reads. It does not require a captured project carrier to have a matching row at sealing, or explicitly represent the absent-carrier case. A disappearing carrier therefore escapes the stated comparison.

**Evidence.** B1's project carrier is optional: M3-B item 2 says absence means no layer. When present, its bytes are read before the C1 session exists. R3 line 110 explicitly keeps them outside that session. The session's leftover-capture check at line 117 consequently does not cover this operational capture. Consider a registered project whose carrier selects a project-layer policy or budget: X2 captures and rechecks it under the fence, B1 resolves it, the handoff releases the fence, and the carrier is then removed before the source walk enumerates the root. No source read occurs for that path, so the prescribed digest comparison never occurs; B1's resolved configuration can still be bound with a source inventory lacking its supposedly retained repository carrier. Mandatory X2 checks during its admission do not define this later sealing join. The converse, absent during resolution and created before the walk, also needs an explicit disposition. C1-T25 covers a rewrite, not either presence transition.

**Fix.** Retain the carrier's observed presence with its optional digest, without handing C1 its bytes or changing `MarkerObservation`. At sealing, require the present project carrier to have exactly one source row with the captured digest; require the absent case to agree with the walk's observation. Refuse a presence transition, a digest change, or exclusion of a required present carrier as `SnapshotCarrierChanged`, without retry. State how this interacts with `ignorePaths`. Add present→absent and absent→present controls, plus stable absence. The anchor exclusion for `.opensip/` can remain as proposed.

### C3-R2 — Apply protocol counters to the actual admitted set

**Location:** item 12, lines 484–490, 497–518; C3-T6b, lines 556–561.

**Problem.** The new counters have the right logical units, but their population and timing are not yet sound. The law stops decoding and refuses the whole import when accumulated content crosses a set counter, while activation and final set admission occur only after supplied sources are materialized and Cargo runs. Those early bytes can belong to an inactive package or to an archive subsequently rejected by the profile. They are not necessarily bytes of the admitted dependency set.

**Evidence.** The pinned reference `native_evidence_model.v2.py:1832–1845` iterates `activated`, skips path packages, and only then selects supplied rows. Its admitted package records at lines 1870–1883, including `fileCount` and `totalBytes`, are built from that population. R3 instead orders materialization of every supplied package, adapter execution, then activated-set admission at lines 515–518. Its line 490 requires the set refusal during decoding. An in-profile activated package with one content byte plus an in-profile, inactive supplied package with 8 GiB produces an early 8 GiB + 1 tally, although the actual admitted set contains one byte. Likewise, content from an archive that later fails its gzip trailer or tar profile must not remain in the final admitted-set tally: lines 500–506 expressly say that package is not admitted and takes the missing/undecodable disposition. [arithmetic_checks.json](arithmetic_checks.json) records the first counterexample as integer arithmetic; no archive was constructed or decoded.

**Fix.** Distinguish provisional acquisition/decoder accounting from protocol totals. Commit per-package logical counts only after successful package admission and use the activated, non-path set population for final protocol counters before its descriptor or transport is published. An early refusal needs a proven overflow of that actual population, not the total of all supplied candidates. If decoding/materialization needs an independent resource cap before activation, give it an owner, quantity and disposition separate from `DependencySetBound`. Add inactive in-profile excess and late outside-profile rejection controls alongside the existing active-set boundary cases.

## Non-blocking observations

**C3-N1 — Snapshot ledger pricing needs a corrected derivation.** Item 2 line 142 describes 2^33 bytes as the 8 GiB total “plus” metadata, but 2^33 is exactly 8 GiB. Also, `WorkCost.bytes` prices requested storage, not just file content: product `work_reader.rs:26–54,158–172` charges cumulative buffer growth, and `filesystem/path_binding.rs:117–126` and `descriptor_acl_capture.rs:111–124` price metadata/capture buffers separately. A 4 MiB bounded read has 12,578,817 bytes of growth charges. This does not prove C1 must exhaust its proposed cap: a streaming owner may use a different pricing recipe. Specify that recipe and derive its cap from all charged buffers, copies and metadata; extend the byte evidence as well as the object/edge census and C1-T20. Do not treat the payload bound alone as a ledger-cost proof.

**C3-N2 — Tighten decoder evidence and boundary wording.** D3 line 494's stored-block ratio is not a universal DEFLATE bound: blocks may be shorter than 65,535 bytes. The claimed producer margin should come from R6's pinned compressor/configuration and its streaming behavior. [RFC 1951 §§2 and 3.2.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1951) permits arbitrary block sizes and describes the stored-block fields; it does not require full-sized stored blocks. No legitimate Cargo counterexample to D3 was established here. Also, a 16,386-byte `L` payload violates D2, but pads to the same 16,896 bytes as 16,385, so it is not by itself the stated one-byte-over-D1 example. Give D1 its own crossing control, and qualify the retained C3-T6 sentence at line 554 so only a genuine protocol-content overflow refuses the set.

**C3-N3 — Keep capture provenance outside the closed inventory.** Item 1 lines 118 and 134 say every inventory row carries an origin. Clarify that this is a host operational side record/projected trace, while the identity row remains exactly `{path, sha256, bytes}` (`identity-schemas.v3.json` source-inventory/Blob). The origin must not enter `snapshot2` or its inventory digest. Likewise, narrow the older “any read of project bytes” prohibition and “only project-file read site” control at lines 123 and 126 to source reads, consistent with r3's expressly permitted operational reads/rechecks.
