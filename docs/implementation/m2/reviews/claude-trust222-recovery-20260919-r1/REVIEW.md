## 1. One owner for a proven rollback — evaluation and recommendation

**What the census actually holds. [E, 222 r3]** A publication descriptor's `afterProjection` is "the complete prospective capsule with ONLY its `publication` member omitted". So for every proven-committed revision the **exact capsule bytes are reconstructible** and hash-checkable against the successor's `previousCapsule`; with R-1 they were *durably* current. Everything that capsule references (events, private records, signed objects) is immutable and never collected. The lost state is therefore not three integers: it is a complete, locally constructed, byte-exact trust closure — F/L/anchor, serial, six counters, head identities, history head, role records, `sourceFence`.

**Option (a) — refusal-only, remedy elsewhere.** Simplest to state, but the remedy it points to (authorized restoration/adoption of a coherent full closure) has no owner today, and the only existing fallback, a fresh installation, discards *every* protection the detection was meant to defend. Cost: from the moment of detection the install has no lawful path forward. I do not recommend it as the primary rule; it remains the correct **fallback** when (b)'s preconditions fail.

**Option (b) — recommended, as an explicit amendment of "detection-only / no adoption".** `acknowledge-restore` is the single owner. When its census proves a rollback it does not reset the observed capsule; it publishes the declared reset **on top of the greatest proven revision**, using the continuity law 222 already has.

*Preconditions (all under the fence; any failure ⇒ fallback, §2):*
1. **Unique proven chain.** From the observed capsule `n` there is exactly one chain of proven-committed revisions `n+1 … N` (each `D(i+1).previousCapsule` = hash of the capsule reconstructed from `D(i)`; same store; adjacent revisions). If any revision on the chain has **two proven successors**, the history is forked and nothing is selected — never by numeric maximum. Unproven descriptors (including the one that proves `N`) are ignored; the one-revision gap stays a disclosed limit.
2. **Complete closure re-admission of `N`.** Every dependency of capsule `N` admits as for a continuity source image: root ancestry and admission nodes, revocation-history DAG within the work profile, head documents under their original contexts, time proof `T`, events. Missing/unadmittable ⇒ unavailable, not a partial adoption.
3. **Monotonicity check, not assumed.** Every floor, counter, serial and F/L of `N` is ≥ the observed capsule's; revocation facts of `n` ⊆ those of `N`; head identities agree wherever versions are equal. This holds by construction when `N` descends from `n`; checking it turns a forged or mis-joined chain into a refusal.

*Effect — no new merge semantics.* Treat proven `N` as the **source image** and the observed capsule as the **target** of the existing same-installation continuity carry (222 "head identity/counter carry", copy/max floors, history `merge` node when heads differ, equal-version different-identity ⇒ refuse). Then apply the declared reset exactly as r3 defines it: every established non-revoked role → `UNBOOTSTRAPPED`/`RESTORED`; `REVOKED` kept; never-established kept; anchor and pending challenge cleared; `sourceFence` kept; **a batch begun in `N` is closed by a private batch-termination (cause `RESTORED`) and staging cleared**; no S4/S5 evaluation; nothing becomes TRUSTED.

*Exact revision/predecessor rules (root's specific concern):*
- New capsule: `revision = N+1`, `previous = SHA-256(capsule N)`, event sequence continues from `N.eventHead`. It is a **logical successor of N**.
- It must **not** pretend the native BEFORE was `N`. The `restore` event and the descriptor carry a separate closed member `nativeBefore = {revision:n, sha256}` plus the ordered `NodeRef`s of the proof chain `D(n+1)…D(N+1)`. The atomic replacement's precondition is *file bytes == `nativeBefore`*; the logical pin is `previous`. The two are never conflated, and an ordinary "n+1" publication cannot be produced by this path because its `operation` kind is `restore-recovery` and `nativeBefore.revision ≠ revision−1` is required (equal ⇒ it is the ordinary r3 reset, with `nativeBefore` = `previous`).
- Why successor-of-`N` rather than successor-of-`n`: after a *second* rollback to `n`, the census then finds the single chain `n…N→N+1'…`; with successor-of-`n` it would find two proven branches at `n` and be forced into the fork refusal forever.
- The pre-existing unproven `D(N+1)` stays an orphan; it and `D'(N+1)` share a predecessor, and only the one that later gains a successor is ever proven. R-1's inference is unaffected: `N` was already proven durable independently of `D'`.

*Why this is safe to amend:* everything adopted is a locally constructed private record, proven durably current, fully re-admitted under original contexts, monotone over the observed state, applied only by an explicit SC-TRUST command, and the result grants **no** standing. An actor able to forge a proven chain in `I/trust/records` can already forge the capsule. *Costs to state:* the one-revision gap; a forked or incomplete history falls back to §2; census and closure work are bounded by the profile.

## 2. Durable *unavailable / unrecoverable* without an unowned flag
Refusing the declaration is the wrong fail-closed direction: nothing forces an operator to acknowledge, so a refused `acknowledge-restore` leaves the rolled-back capsule — possibly with TRUSTED roles — fully operative. The restrictive reset should proceed, **and carry its own obligation**:
- **[P]** one closed capsule member owned by this owner: `restoreDetection: null | {status:"unavailable"|"unrecoverable", by:EventRef}`, written only by the `restore` event of `acknowledge-restore` (`unavailable` = census exhausted or evidence unreadable; `unrecoverable` = proven rollback whose §1 preconditions failed: fork, missing closure, monotonicity failure).
- While non-null, **every TRUSTED-entry and every head/counter-advancing publication** (ordinary import/refresh, ceremony COMMIT, continuity preparation) must, inside its own fenced preflight, re-run the census. Only a **completed clean census** clears the member, in that same outcome revision; a completed census that proves a recoverable rollback refuses with "acknowledge-restore required" (→ §1); still-unavailable or unrecoverable refuses. S4.5, ABORT, diagnostics and a repeated `acknowledge-restore` are not blocked.
- It is not a free-floating flag: single writer, single clearing condition, evidence-bound by `EventRef`, preserved by continuity images like `sourceFence`. An ordinary import can never clear it by succeeding. Honest cost: if the records collection can no longer be censused within the profile, the block is permanent until a reviewed profile successor or the owed adoption owner — which is the stated availability bound, not a silent clearance.

## 3. R-3 — exact time-proof closure
**Accepted by root:** the work profile is an explicit effective cap on recovery-payload metadata.
**Witness producer, checked. [E]** S4: "a witness is the **newest issue time of a presented payload whose signature already verified** and which is not older than L"; A = max(L, accepted witness time, **presented root issue time**). **[E, schemas v2]** the only signed documents that carry `issuedAt` are: the payload manifest (`payload.issuedAt`, TR-BUNDLE), the catalog (TR-INDEX), the revocation list (root threshold) and root documents (a separate term of A). Envelopes and permission policies have **no** `issuedAt`, and the role-member proofs are envelopes. So `newestIssuedAt` can originate only from **three** documents, and a role-member proof can never be a witness source. The reference model and product take `newestIssuedAt` as a scalar input — the producer join is exactly what must be fixed here.
**[P] exact minimal `time-proof` scope** (same closure owner/codec as the ceremony scope, distinct closed scope tag):
1. the **payload manifest** body+envelope — always (it is "the presented payload whose signature already verified", carries `payloadKind`, and binds items 2–3 by digest);
2. the **catalog** body+envelope and the **revocation list** body+envelope — always, not only the arg-max: the witness is a maximum, and re-admission must recompute `max` over the same finite set rather than trust a recorded winner; the list is in any case required for the second-pass key filtering of every signer above;
3. the **root chain** documents from the original authority (`NodeRef` of the admitted core anchor or of the then-current head admission) to the final root — also the source of "presented root issue time";
4. the `NodeRef` of the **prior retained revocation-history node** at evaluation time (the "retained" half of retained∪incoming);
5. schema/domain identifiers and the S4 evaluation inputs (W, M, bootId, prior F/L/anchor) and result.
Bound: ≤ 3 + 64 documents, each ≤ 4 MiB — independent of manifest/envelope cardinality, so ordinary imports are not capped by it. No scalar is self-authenticating: `newestIssuedAt` is **recomputed** from items 1–2 by the existing verifier under items 3–4.
**What this scope does not re-prove, stated plainly:** 215 D/B-6 require that *all relied-on members* authenticated before the payload was used as time evidence. That is an admission-time ordering policy. Its time-relevant content — that no signer of a time-bearing document is revoked by the retained∪incoming view — **is** re-provable from items 1–4. The remainder (that the target roles' member envelopes also verified) signs no time and cannot raise or lower a witness; record it as a closed attestation in the clock event (`reliedOnAuthentication: complete`, with the manifest inventory digest), *recorded, not re-provable*. If root requires that remainder to be re-provable too, the only honest alternative is the complete relied-on closure, and then the profile **does** cap ordinary payload size; I see no security property of L that needs it.
