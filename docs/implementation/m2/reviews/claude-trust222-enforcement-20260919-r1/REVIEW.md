# Bounded reconciliation — enforcement of a detected trust-capsule rollback (alternatives A / B)

Reviewer: Claude. Date 2026-09-19. **Assistance only; no product/root edits, commit, push or approval.** Continues `claude-trust222-recovery-20260919-r1` (unchanged; corrected here). Does not revisit the 215 model and defines no schema. **[E]** retained law · **[P]** proposal. One logical probe: `claude-out/probes/three_revision.py` → `io/three-revision.json`.

Taken as decided by root: R-1 durability reconfirmation before any successor private record; full verified unique proven-state recovery as an explicit `acknowledge-restore` amendment; no pre-rule chain; **complete relied-on authentication closure for the time proof** — so the finite metadata work profile caps **ordinary and recovery** payloads below the structural manifest limits, and that must be documented as such; my `issuedAt` scoping is source reading, not a verified producer law.

## 1. The defect root identified is real — and worse than stated (my §2 flag is withdrawn)
Minimal history: capsules 1, 2, 3 committed; an attempt at 4 was prepared, so descriptors D2, D3, D4 are durable and 2, 3 are proven. The capsule file alone is rolled back to 1; recovery of N = 3 is unavailable. My proposal then published a restrictive capsule 2′ (previous = hash(1)) carrying `restoreDetection`. Probe results:
| Step | Census result |
|---|---|
| after rollback, observed 1 | `behind`, proven chain 2, 3 |
| fallback 2′, then any successor 3′ (S4.5, ABORT, anything) — census from the tip 3′ | **`clean`** |
| a second rollback to 1 | **`FORK` at 1: branches 2 and 2′** |
So (i) *any* later publication proves 2′ — S4.5 and ABORT are not exempt, as root suspected; (ii) my clearing rule was wrong, not merely optimistic: a census looks only at **descendants** of the observed capsule, and the legitimately committed 2..3 is a **sibling** of 2′, so the "completed clean census" would clear the flag while the lost branch still sits on disk; (iii) the install has manufactured a proven fork that makes every future recovery refuse; (iv) 2′ cannot carry the revocation history that lived only in 2..3. The flag is withdrawn.

## 2. Alternative B — can it be made sound? Only by building a branch-merge system
To avoid (ii)–(iv) the obligation would have to name the lost chain (`lostChain:[D2..D4]`), be clearable only by a later successful recovery onto N, and that recovery would now have **two** predecessors (logical N, native 3′) and must join them; the census would need a new *supersession* rule ("a proven branch whose tip is named as `nativeBefore` by a proven restore-recovery descriptor on the other branch is not a fork"), and a crash between the restrictive declaration and the later recovery leaves exactly the two-proven-branch world that rule must then always resolve. Each piece is definable; together they are a version-control merge protocol inside the trust store, all to preserve one property — that a *failed* acknowledgement still restricts. **I do not recommend B.**

## 3. Recommendation: **A**, with one structural refinement that removes its major cost
A's own rule, stated once: **no capsule is ever published as the successor of a capsule that has a proven successor.** The only exception is §1-recovery of my previous note, which publishes as the successor of **N**, not of the observed capsule. Consequences: the system can never manufacture a proven fork (the probe's A case refuses publication at 1); a failed `acknowledge-restore` is a refusal and writes nothing; **the durable obligation is the evidence itself** — as long as the proving descriptors are on disk every check re-derives the denial, with no flag, side file or pointer; if that evidence later disappears the situation *is* the coherent-rollback case retained law already declares undetected.

**Independent objection to A as drafted (lifetime scan at every authority use).** It is not viable, for a reason beyond cost: the records collection only grows, the census is budgeted (65,536 objects), and "failure denies authority". A perfectly healthy install therefore crosses the budget by ordinary use and then denies **all** trust authority permanently. A mandatory full-directory census cannot be the enforcement mechanism at any frequency above "rare explicit command".

**Does the threat model require a check at every authority use at all?** **[E]** v8 §5.5 lists "a trust store restored below any document still on disk" under *Detected*, v1 says such a store "is quarantined and the install refuses", and neither says **when** detection happens; v8 also states "No origin check is performed before offline operations" and accepts that an undetected rollback "may reuse the authority it validly held at its own time". Against a deliberate actor the detection has no force — whoever can replace the capsule can also remove the records ("marker suppressed") — so what it actually covers is an *incoherent* restore (backup tooling, operator error). Two honest positions follow: (1) check only at `acknowledge-restore` and explicit diagnosis, and **amend the claim** to "detected when declared or diagnosed; an undeclared partial restore is out of scope" — lawful only as an explicit narrowing of v1/v8 wording, which I would not make silently; or (2) keep the retained claim and make the check cheap enough to run always. I recommend (2), because it needs only a locator change:

**[P] Predecessor-keyed descriptor locator (A′).** A publication descriptor is published at `I/trust/records/successors/<sha256 of previousCapsule>/<sha256 of descriptor>` (first capsule of a store: keyed by its store identity). It is the same immutable, internally constructed, exclusive-no-replace private record as today — not a pointer, not a high-water mark, not mutable, no new owner; the capsule's `publication` reference carries `{previousCapsule, sha256, bytes}` so the path is derived, never searched. Then:
- *Check* (under the fence, before any trust-authority use that r3 already fences, and before **every** successor publication, immediately after R-1): admit `successors/<hash(current)>`. Absent ⇒ clean. Present ⇒ for each entry (bounded count), admit the descriptor, reconstruct its capsule, and admit `successors/<hash(that capsule)>`; an admitted entry there proves it. No proven successor ⇒ clean (entries are crash orphans). Exactly one ⇒ **behind**: deny authority, refuse every publication except §1-recovery. More than one ⇒ fork: deny, refuse, no selection. Unreadable/unadmittable directory or entry ⇒ unavailable: deny and refuse; never clean.
- *Cost, finite:* healthy tip = **one** directory admission (probe: 0 entries examined vs 24 for the scan on the same history); after k crashed attempts from the same predecessor, k descriptor admissions (cap the entries examined per directory, e.g. 64, and the chain length followed, e.g. 4,096; exhaustion ⇒ unavailable). Write side: one directory creation + parent barrier per revision; `successors/` gains one subdirectory per revision for the life of the store (no GC) — shard by hash prefix if a flat directory is a concern. Read-only callers perform the same reads and write nothing.
- *Recovery* (§1 of my previous note) is unchanged and becomes cheaper: it walks `successors/` from the observed capsule instead of scanning, and the fork test is local to each directory.

**Precise laws, collected.**
1. R-1, then the successor check, precede every private record of a new revision.
2. No successor of a capsule with a proven successor, except `restore-recovery`, which succeeds **N** (`previous = hash(N)`, `revision = N+1`, separate `nativeBefore`).
3. `acknowledge-restore`: clean ⇒ ordinary one-capsule reset; behind + unique + complete closure + monotone ⇒ recovery; otherwise **refusal with no write**, and authority stays denied by law 4.
4. While the check yields behind / fork / unavailable, all trust authority is denied, including roles whose token is `TRUSTED`; S4.5 and ABORT are publications and are refused too (they would prove a sibling); diagnostics report, write-free.
5. Nothing is ever selected, raised or replayed from an unproven descriptor.

## 4. Exact unresolved limits
- **One-revision gap.** A rollback by exactly one revision is indistinguishable from a crash orphan; the install then publishes (n+1)′, and the lost n+1 stays unproven forever — no fork, but no detection. Closing it needs evidence written *after* the capsule barrier, which is a new owner; not proposed.
- **Coherent rollback** of capsule and records together: undetected, as retained law states.
- **Unrecoverable history** (fork, or N's closure unadmittable): authority is denied until the owed creator/adoption owner exists; there is deliberately no restrictive fallback. That is A's availability cost, accepted with it.
- **Denial scope** depends on which authority uses are fenced today; an unfenced consumer (if any exists) would not run the check — this note claims nothing about them.
- Everything rests on R-1 holding for every revision on the chain and on `I/trust/records` custody; an actor who can write there can forge the capsule as well.
- Logical reasoning and one logical probe only; no filesystem, crash or native qualification; no model of A′ exists yet. Minimum model rows: the three-revision history above; orphan-only successor directory; two orphans; proven fork; unreadable entry; recovery followed by a second rollback (must yield one chain through the recovery capsule).
